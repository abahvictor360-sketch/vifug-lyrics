A new volunteer sits down at the media desk, the pastor says "just follow along", and ten minutes later the wrong verse is on the wall. Nobody is to blame. The volunteer was never shown the order of a service, never practised pressing the buttons, and never knew who to ask. Most media-team trouble comes from skipping the training and hoping a keen person figures it out under pressure.

This is a simple four-week plan that works for a small team with limited time. It assumes people are volunteers with day jobs, so each step is small and repeatable.

Decide the roles first

New people learn faster when the job has edges. Even in a team of two, name the roles, then let one person hold more than one when needed.

RoleWhat they doGood first role?
Slides and lyrics operatorRuns the words on screen: cues, previews, sends liveYes, after shadowing
Sound operatorMixes the room and any recorded audioOnly with separate training
Camera or stream operatorShoots or switches video, monitors the streamLater, once screens are comfortable
Team lead on the dayArrives first, runs the check, makes the call if something breaksAn experienced person, not a newcomer

Start newcomers on the words. It is the most visible job, the mistakes are the easiest to recover from, and it teaches the order of the service better than any other seat.

Week 1: watch and learn the service

In the first week, the new volunteer does not touch anything live. They sit beside whoever is operating and follow the service from the plan.

  • Show them the run sheet and talk through why items are in that order.
  • Explain the vocabulary: what Preview means, what Live means, what blanking the screen is for.
  • Let them hold the printed checklist (below) and tick things off as they happen.
  • After the service, spend ten minutes asking what surprised them.

The goal is that by Sunday evening they can describe the service in their own words and name the three things that can go wrong most often in your church.

Week 2: practise on a weekday

Pick a weekday evening and an empty room. The volunteer sits at the desk with the real equipment and the real software, and runs a pretend service from a run sheet you hand them.

  1. Cue a song, check it in Preview, then send it live. Repeat until the rhythm feels automatic.
  2. Find and display a Bible reference they have not seen before.
  3. Show a PowerPoint or PDF from the presentations list.
  4. Blank the screen for prayer, then bring the words back.
  5. Deliberately do something wrong, such as sending the wrong song live, and recover from it. The first time should not be on a Sunday.

Making the mistake in an empty room removes most of the fear. Let them make three or four.

Week 3: run part of a real service

Now they operate a section of Sunday, with the experienced person sitting next to them. A good split is the worship set, since lyrics are predictable and the order is already known. The mentor's only job is to speak up early, never to grab the mouse. If something goes wrong, the mentor talks them through the fix and afterwards says what they would do next time.

At this stage it helps to give the volunteer a small set of shortcuts to remember rather than the whole menu. Week 4: run the whole service, mentor nearby

The volunteer operates the full service. The mentor is in the room but not at the desk. Afterwards, hold a short debrief with three questions: what went well, what was confusing, what should we change on the checklist. Then put them on the rota. A person who has run one full service with support is ready to be scheduled alongside others.

Keep a back-up plan for their first solo Sunday. Tell them who to text if they get stuck, and make sure that person has their phone on.

The one-page checklist

A single printed page beats a thick manual. Put it at the desk, in a plastic sleeve. Here is a starting version to adapt:

  1. Arrive 45 minutes early. Turn on the computer, projector and any other screens.
  2. Open the software and load today's service plan.
  3. Confirm the projector is showing the output on the correct screen.
  4. Put the welcome or logo slide on the wall.
  5. Check the first song, the Scripture and the announcements are in order and spelled correctly.
  6. Check the stage display and any other screens are on.
  7. Test audio if you are playing anything from the computer.
  8. Meet the worship leader and pastor. Ask: any changes to the order?
  9. Check the phone remote connects, if you use one.
  10. After the service: blank the screen, close the output, shut down properly, and put the cables back.

Update the page whenever something goes wrong. If a new problem happens twice, it belongs on the list.

Rehearsal habits that pay off

  • Run through the plan on Saturday or on Sunday before people arrive, not only on the live morning.
  • Always check slides in Preview before sending them live. The Preview and Live workflow exists so the congregation never sees a typo, and it only works if you actually look.
  • Keep a written note of every last-minute change and who requested it, so the next operator does not repeat the confusion.
  • Practise the awkward moments: a late song request, a missing Scripture reference, a song with more verses than usual.
  • Swap seats now and then so more than one person can run the words.

Calm Sunday-morning routines

Calm is mostly preparation. A few habits help nervous volunteers more than any technique:

  • Plan to be finished with setup before the first person arrives.
  • Do not change the plan in the last ten minutes unless someone with authority asks.
  • Say out loud what you are about to do: "next up, song two". It helps both the team and your own focus.
  • If you make a mistake, blank the screen, fix it, then continue. Nobody remembers a two-second blank, but they remember a panic.
  • Keep a bottle of water and a printed copy of the run sheet beside you.

Keep the team healthy

Training does not end at week four. A volunteer who serves every Sunday for a year with no break will quietly burn out, and a team of one is a single point of failure for the whole service.

  • Rotate. Two Sundays on, one off works for many teams, and it forces the knowledge to be shared.
  • Keep the checklist and run sheet where everyone can find them, not on one person's laptop.
  • Thank people by name, in front of others, for specific things: "the Scripture was up before he finished reading it".
  • Meet for fifteen minutes once a month. Ask what is annoying, what is confusing and what nearly went wrong.
  • Invite one younger or newer person to shadow each experienced operator. That is next year's team.
