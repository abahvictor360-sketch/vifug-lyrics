The worship leader turns around halfway through the bridge to read the lyrics off the wall behind her. The preacher has no idea how long he has left. A family in the overflow room is watching a song with a countdown timer drawn across it that was meant for the platform. All three are the same mistake: one picture sent to every screen, when each screen has a different audience.

Once a church has more than a single projector, the real job is deciding what each screen is for. This post covers the usual kinds of screen, what each should show, and how to set that up in Vifug.

Know your audiences

Before touching any settings, write down every place a person looks at a screen during your service, and what they need from it. A typical list looks like this:

ScreenWho watchesWhat it should show
Main projector or TVThe congregationLyrics, Scripture, slides, announcements
Confidence monitor (stage display)Worship team, preacher, readersCurrent line, next line, a clock, notes
Overflow roomPeople watching from a second roomWhat the main room sees, sometimes with extra context
Lobby or foyerLatecomers, parents, volunteers on the doorA welcome slide, a countdown to the next service, notices
Stream or onlineViewers at homeLyrics as a lower third over the camera, or full-screen words

You do not need all of these. A small church might have just the first two. The point of the table is to stop you reaching for "mirror everything" by default.

What a good confidence monitor shows

A confidence monitor, sometimes called a stage monitor or stage display, faces the people on the platform. Its job is to let them stop looking at the back wall. The things that make it useful are fairly consistent:

  • Current and next. Singers need to see the next line before it arrives. Seeing only the current slide means they are always one beat behind.
  • A clock. The preacher and the host both want to know the time without checking a watch.
  • Notes. Cues like "pray after this song" or "offering next" belong here, not on the congregation's screen.
  • Large, high-contrast text. People read it from several metres away while singing or holding an instrument. Aim for text that is clearly bigger than what you would put on a handheld.
  • Placement. Put it where the leader's eyes already go, at the back of the room or on the floor in front of the platform, angled up. Not behind the drummer.

It does not need to be fancy. An old TV on a stand, with a cable that reaches it, is enough.

Lobby and overflow screens

A lobby screen has different rules. People pass it, they do not stand and stare. A big readable welcome, the service time, and one or two notices will do far more than live lyrics at small size. If you want latecomers to feel a service is in progress without hearing it, a muted view of the main output works. But a plain "Welcome, service continues in the main hall" slide is often kinder to someone juggling a baby and a coat.

An overflow room usually does want the main output, since the people there are part of the service. Two small additions help: check the audio feed is correct, and decide whether they need the same lower-third Scripture treatment the stream uses.

  • Test each of these screens at the position people will actually sit or stand.
  • Give each screen a name that a volunteer will recognise, such as "Foyer" or "Band".
  • Keep a written list of which device or monitor each name refers to.

Setting up extra screens in Vifug

The main output in Vifug is the one every church has, the projector or TV. Anything beyond that is an extra screen. You add these in Settings, then Streaming and output, then Extra screens, as described in the projection chapter of the guide. Give each one a name, and you then get two ways to put it somewhere:

  1. A monitor on this machine. Pick the display and press Open window. Vifug opens a full-screen output on a monitor that no other screen is already using.
  2. Any other device. Copy link gives you the screen's address. Open it in a browser on a smart TV, a tablet or a laptop on the same network. A screen is only ever an address, which is why the same feature covers a second projector and a phone taped to a music stand.

Removing a screen from the list also closes its window, so you are not left with a black rectangle on a monitor that nothing controls.

Different content on each screen

Having the screens is half of it. The other half is choosing what goes where, and Vifug is built so you cannot easily put the wrong thing in front of the congregation.

To send something to a particular extra screen, cue it into Preview as usual. Then right-click the Preview thumbnail and choose the screen under Send preview to. Only that screen changes. The main output carries on with whatever is already on it, and GO LIVE still means the main output and nothing else.

The Every screen chapter adds two other routes:

  • For a picture or video, open the Media tab, click the item, and use Send to a screen in the panel beside the grid.
  • For a presentation slide, open the Presentations tab, click the slide so it is cued in Preview, then use Send to a screen above the slides. It sends the slide that is cued.

Each extra screen has a row with Send and Clear. Send puts the content on that screen only, and the row says sent so you can see where it went. Clear empties just that screen. The main screen is not in that list, because GO LIVE already is the main screen.

The stage display

Vifug's stage display is a separate page meant for the platform. Per the guide, it shows what is on now, the clock and what comes next, plus your service notes. You open the stage address on a screen facing the worship team. The companion addresses, including the remote link, are listed in Settings, Streaming and output, under Outputs and companion screens.

Because it is a web address, it works on anything with a browser on the same network: an old laptop, a tablet on a stand, a TV with a browser. If it will not load on another device, the cause is nearly always the computer's firewall, which the guide explains in its chapter on letting phones and tablets connect.

Pair it with the countdown timer when the preacher wants to see time left. In the Timer panel, the Show it on choices let you put the countdown on the Main screen, the Stage, the Stream, or any combination. A timer meant for the platform belongs on the Stage only. The congregation does not need to watch the clock run down on the speaker.

Per-screen themes

Different audiences also want different looks. White text over a busy photo works on the main wall but is hard to read on a small stage monitor. Vifug lets each screen wear its own theme. Open Settings, then Lyrics, then Theme for each screen. The top rows pick the kind of content (lyrics, Scripture, presentations) and the bottom rows pick the audience: the stream overlay and each extra screen, such as stage, lobby or overflow. Leave a row on Same as lyrics to follow the main look, or give it a theme of its own.

A sensible starting point:

  • Stage: plain dark background, very large white text, no motion video.
  • Lobby: your church logo or a welcome background, a theme with a bar behind the words so a notice stays legible.
  • Overflow: same as the main room.

The guide notes that screens pick up theme changes within about four seconds, so after editing, wait a moment before deciding it did not work.

A pre-service screen check

  1. Main screen shows the welcome or logo slide.
  2. Stage display is open on the monitor facing the team, showing the clock.
  3. Lobby screen shows its own welcome slide, not the main output.
  4. Send a test slide to each extra screen and clear it again.
  5. Check the timer is set to show only where you want it.

Trying it yourself

If you want to set this up without cost, Vifug is free to download and the extra screens, stage display and per-screen themes are all part of the app. Start with the main projector, as in setting up a projector and second screen, then add one extra screen at a time.
