Presenting Bible verses well on screen

Practical advice on translation choice, verse chunking, references, multiple languages and pace, with an offline Bible in 31 English versions and more.

A verse is on the screen, the preacher has started reading, and half the room is still finishing the sentence before. Or the passage was too long and the font shrank until nobody past row four could read it. Or the reference showed up in tiny letters and three people asked "where is that?" Putting Scripture on a wall looks like the simplest job in the media booth, and it is the one people notice most when it goes wrong.

Here is a practical approach to translation, chunking, references, languages and pace, with Vifug's Bible tab as the working example.

Choose a translation on purpose

Most churches have a default Bible, and it should match what the preacher reads from, as closely as you can manage. If the pastor preaches from one translation and the screen shows another, people will hear one thing and read another, and some will assume you made a mistake.

Some practical rules:

  • Agree one default version with the preaching team, and tell the media team what it is.
  • If a guest speaker brings a different translation, ask in advance and switch for that talk.
  • Remember licensing. Many modern translations are copyrighted, and your church may need a licence to display them. Only add a translation of your own if you have the right to use it.
  • For a mixed congregation, keep a simple, readable version for the screen even if the pastor reads from a more literal one.

Chunk verses so they can be read

The hardest decision is how much text goes on one slide. A few guidelines that hold up in most rooms:

  • One verse per slide is a good default. Two short verses can share a slide if they form one thought.
  • A long verse is better split across two slides than shrunk. Aim for no more than four to five lines at a size that reads from the back.
  • Break at natural pauses, at a comma or a clause, not in the middle of a phrase.
  • Do not cram a whole paragraph onto a slide. People read at different speeds, and a wall of text invites them to read ahead of the preacher.

It helps to test your own back row. Stand at the far end of the room, with the text at the size you use, and read a long verse. If it takes effort, the font is too small or the passage is too long.

Make the reference easy to find

A verse with no reference is a puzzle. Always show the book, chapter, verse and translation, for example John 3:16 (KJV). Vifug puts the reference and translation under the verse. With the Clean card theme, the reference can be moved: Left, Centre or Right, and above or below the verse.

A few habits help people follow along:

  • Put the reference where it can be read first, or at least consistently in the same place.
  • For a series of verses, show the range, not each reference separately, if the preacher reads through a passage.
  • Pre-cue the next reference while the current verse is live. In Vifug the preview pane shows what the congregation will see before you press Go live.

Multiple languages

Many congregations are not monolingual. A few options, in rough order of effort:

  1. Switch the version for the whole service. Simple when the room mostly speaks one language.
  2. Show the Scripture in the main language and keep a printed or phone copy in another. Low effort, but the screen helps only some people.
  3. Show two languages together. Vifug has a translation line, where a second-language line is added to each section, and the guide describes a dual-language display with its own colour. That is documented for song lyrics, so check the guide before assuming the same layout for Scripture.

When languages share the screen, give each its own consistent colour or position so people find their line quickly, and make the text a little smaller so both fit. If the room is split, consider whether a second screen or a stage screen for the translator is cleaner than one crowded slide.

Pace and rhythm

A verse should appear slightly before it is read, not after. If it arrives late, the preacher has already moved on, and the room has lost their place. If it arrives too early on a long passage, people read ahead and drift.

  • Cue the verse during the sentence that introduces it.
  • Advance on the preacher's cue, not a timer. A reading has its own rhythm.
  • For a long reading, move one verse at a time and keep the next one in Preview.
  • Blank the screen between passages rather than leaving a verse up after the preacher has moved on.

Vifug's AI auto-follow can put up a verse when it hears a spoken reference, and the safest setting is to send it to Preview so you press Go live yourself. It is an assistant, not a replacement for a person watching the room.

A pre-service checklist

  • Confirm the translation with the preacher.
  • Pre-load the passages that are known in advance.
  • Check the text margin on the actual screen so the edges are not cropped.
  • Look at the longest verse of the day at the back of the room.
  • Test the reference position and size, and have one operator who knows the shortcut keys.
